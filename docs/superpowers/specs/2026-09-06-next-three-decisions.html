01 Blocks the + button on every screen

What happens when someone wants to make a plan themselves?

The green + sits on every screen and goes nowhere. It's not a small gap: friend-initiated proposals are one of the two card types in the feed — “Jeff wants to hang out” has to come from somewhere. And this is the decision that defines what Spont is, because the moment you let someone pick a time by hand, you've built a calendar invite with extra steps.

A Pick the person, not the time

You choose who. Spont finds the slots and sends them a proposal, exactly like a system suggestion but initiated by you.

Cost: you can't say “Thursday at 8” when you already know you mean Thursday at 8.

B Full composer

Pick who, pick a day, pick a time, send. The familiar thing everyone already knows how to use.

Cost: it's a calendar invite. The scheduling engine becomes optional, and the product's whole reason to exist becomes optional with it.

C Just raise a hand

You can't propose a time at all — only tell Spont “I want to see Rod soon,” which weights him up in the engine. A wish, not an invite.

Cost: nothing happens immediately. People used to sending invites will find it maddening at first.

D A, with B as an escape hatch

Spont picks by default; a “pick it myself” link underneath for when you genuinely already know.

Cost: escape hatches get used. Whatever's behind that link becomes the real flow for your power users.

✓

I'd take A, with C bolted on as a second action. “Pick the person, we'll find the time” is the same promise the feed already makes, so it doesn't need a second mental model — and “raise a hand” covers the real case where you're not proposing anything, you just miss someone. D is the safe choice, and I'd expect the escape hatch to quietly eat the product.

02 The most-seen screen in the app

What does the feed look like when there's nothing in it?

There's a cap of one proposal per day, deliberately. That means a near-empty feed isn't an edge case — it's the normal state, and it's what most people will see most times they open the app. It's been flagged as undesigned since the original home-feed notes, and right now it's a placeholder sentence I wrote so the filters wouldn't break. Tap “Today” in the mockup and you're looking at the gap.

A Say it plainly

“Nothing open right now.” A quiet line, one action underneath, no decoration.

Cost: honest but flat. Reads as the app having failed to find you anything.

B Never let it be empty

Backfill with what's already locked in, someone you haven't seen in a while, or a prompt to raise a hand.

Cost: manufactured activity. This is the road to a feed that's busy because it needs to be, not because anything happened.

C Empty is the win

Frame it as success: nothing to decide, you're set, three things already on the calendar. Close the app.

Cost: requires nerve. An app that congratulates you for leaving is measured differently than one that keeps you.

D Show the near miss

“You and Edward were both nearly free Thursday.” Explain what the engine tried, so quiet reads as working, not broken.

Cost: exposes free/busy inference in a way the privacy line may not comfortably cover.

✓

C, structured like A. You've now said twice that the goal is less time in the app — an empty feed is that goal being met, and it's the one screen where you can prove the mission instead of asserting it. Keep it plain, keep one action available, and don't backfill. B is the version of this app I'd worry about becoming.

03 Design and roadmap currently disagree

Does anyone get to tune how Spont picks?

This one's a genuine contradiction, not an open question. The foundation spec commits Phase 3 to “user-configurable, equally-weighted optimization signals” — recency, time-of-day preference, buffer around events, category balance — and names Settings as their home. The home-feed design went the other way and cut configuration entirely as a deliberate YAGNI call. Both can't ship. Phase 3 is the next real build after the label work, so this gets decided soon whether or not it gets decided on purpose.

A No controls at all

Spont decides, full stop. Amend the spec to drop configurability.

Cost: when suggestions are wrong, there's no recourse but declining forever. And the engine never learns why.

B Build the settings panel

Four signals, four controls, as the spec describes. Honest and explicit.

Cost: a config screen in an app whose stated goal is that you don't open it. Almost nobody will touch it, and it'll shape the engine for the few who do.

C Learn from the decisions

No controls. The engine reads your accepts and declines — decline every 8am and it stops offering 8am.

Cost: invisible and slow. Declining for one reason teaches the machine another, and nobody can see or correct what it concluded.

D Ask in the moment

No settings page. Occasionally, on decline: “too early?” One tap, and the signal is set where the context actually is.

Cost: asking costs attention every time. Overdone, it turns every no into a survey.

✓

D on top of C. It satisfies what Phase 3 actually needs — signals that reflect the person — without building the settings screen this product shouldn't want. It also gives the decline button a second job, which matters now that declining is the only feedback the engine gets. Whichever way you go, the foundation spec needs amending to match, and that's worth doing explicitly rather than letting Phase 3 discover it.

Also open, deliberately not in the top three

  • How exposed is a low rank? You've decided rank is visible to friends, but not whether Rod sees that he reads as “Flaky.” It's the riskiest call in the product — it's just Phase 6, so it has time.
  • Categories are about to exist. Phase 2 builds a label taxonomy with privacy-scoped sharing. Cards cut category because there was no data for it; that reason expires next phase, and it's worth deciding before the data arrives rather than after.
  • Where do card photos come from? Still placeholder art. Camera-roll access is a permission scope no phase currently covers.
  • Nothing happens when you tap “I'm in.” The confirmation state got removed along with the flake experiment. Phase 4 writes to your calendar instantly — that deserves a visible moment.
  • The connect screen runs eleven seconds for a one-second job. Either hold it deliberately or cut to success after two lines.
  • design-principles.md still describes the old system. Today's direction contradicts it on shape, colour and type. Either rewrite it around this, or call the pivot an experiment — but not by default.
