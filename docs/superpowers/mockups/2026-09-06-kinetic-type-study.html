Eight more, on the artistic end.

Where the first six treated the line as a block that arrives, these treat the letters themselves as the material. Each panel loops two taglines so you can see the hand-off as well as the entrance.

DecodeSearching

Characters churn through noise and lock in left to right. The only one that literally looks like the app resolving an answer — a bit techy, very on-message.

Split-flapDeparture board

Each letter flips down like an airport board. Scheduling has always looked like this — it's the most thematically earned of the eight.

Tracking collapseTypographic

Letters start spread wide and tighten into the line. Pure type craft — no gimmick, reads as design rather than effect. My pick for restraint.

OverprintTwo into one

Two misregistered copies slide into alignment. It's the product as a printing metaphor: two schedules, offset, coming into register.

ScatterAssembling

Characters fly in from everywhere and find their seats. Playful and a little chaotic — closest to the warmth of the copy, furthest from calm.

Slit scanGraphic

A band opens from the centre line and closes again. Cinematic and cheap to run — nothing here can stutter on a slow phone.

Weight bloomVariable font

The line arrives thin and gains body as it settles. Subtle enough that people feel it rather than notice it. Needs the variable weight axis.

StackAccumulating

Lines pile up instead of replacing each other — earlier ones shrink and fade upward. The only option where someone can read all five, which fixes the timing problem outright.

Two of these solve problems, not just decorate. Stack lets all five lines coexist, so an eleven-second script stops being eleven seconds of waiting. Decode and Split-flap both make the waiting itself legible — the screen looks like it's working, which buys patience the other treatments have to ask for.
