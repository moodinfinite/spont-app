Six ways the lines could move.

Same copy, same typeface, same green as the real connect screen — only the motion changes. Each panel loops two of the five taglines so you can judge the hand-off between lines, not just the entrance.

Rise & fadeCurrent

Quiet and safe. Reads as an app doing a job — which is fine, but it's the one nobody will remember.

Word by wordOption

Lands like a thought forming. Best fit for lines with a turn in them — “Less this. More that.” Costs ~300ms more per line.

Blur focusOption

Soft, filmic, expensive-feeling. Suits “hiding in your calendar” — something resolving into view. Watch it on cheap Android; blur can stutter.

Slot rollOption

Departure-board energy. The one that most feels like a machine searching — which is literally what's happening while it connects.

Wipe revealOption

Graphic and confident, closest to a title card. Strongest with short lines; long ones take a beat too many to uncover.

TypedOption

Most human, most overused. Slow by nature — you'd get through two lines, not five. Fits if the voice is meant to feel like a friend texting.

The timing question underneath all of these: a real calendar connect takes about a second, and five lines at ~2.3s each is eleven. Either the screen holds deliberately longer than the work needs, or you show one or two lines and cut to the success state. Whichever motion you pick, that's the call that decides how many of the five anyone actually reads.
