What the + does.

The green + sits on every screen and currently opens a half-built create flow. The question underneath is what “add” means in an app that schedules for you: a person, a plan, or a wish. These are four answers.

1 · Ask for a menu firstSafe

What are you adding?

Three things live behind this button.

A hangoutPick who — we’ll find the time
A friendSend someone your invite link
A groupA crew you see together

Honest about the three jobs, and nothing is buried. But it puts a decision in front of the action every single time — a menu is a tax on the common case.

2 · Straight to whoCurrent direction

Who do you want to see?

Pick one. We’ll handle the when.

One screen, one job, no menu. Adding a friend or group moves to the People page where it arguably belongs. The + becomes unambiguous: it means “I want to see someone.”

3 · Raise a handMost on-mission

Who are you missing?

No time, no invite — just tell Spont who to prioritise. It’ll surface them the moment your calendars allow.

Never sends anything, so it can’t be declined or ignored — it only weights the engine. Nothing happens immediately, which will madden anyone expecting to send an invite.

Gives people the steering they'll ask for without letting them pick an exact slot the other person may not have free. A middle path between “you decide” and “I decide.”

My pick is 2, with 3 as a second action inside it. The + should mean one thing, and “I want to see someone” is the only meaning that isn't already served elsewhere — adding friends belongs on People, groups belong on the Groups tab. Option 4 is what people will ask for after a week of use; worth keeping in a drawer rather than building now. Option 1 is the safe choice that quietly admits the button doesn't know what it's for.
